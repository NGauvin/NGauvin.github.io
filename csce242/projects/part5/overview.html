Finance Overview

Before diving into formulas and instruments, we must understand what finance is, why it exists, and the role it plays in the economy.

1

What is Finance?

At its core, finance is the science of managing money across time. It encompasses the decisions individuals, businesses, and governments make about acquiring and deploying capital to achieve their objectives.

Finance asks three fundamental questions:

Where does money come from?

How capital is raised — through debt, equity, or retained earnings.

Where does money go?

How capital is deployed — which projects, assets, or investments receive funding.

How is value created?

Whether the returns generated justify the risks and costs of the capital deployed.

Three Domains of Finance

Personal Finance

Managing individual income, savings, investment, and debt; the decisions you make every day.

  • Budgeting
  • Retirement savings
  • Mortgages
  • Tax planning

Corporate Finance

How companies raise capital, allocate resources, and maximize shareholder value.

  • Capital structure
  • Mergers & acquisitions
  • Dividend policy
  • Valuation

Capital Markets

The markets where buyers and sellers trade financial instruments like stocks and bonds.

  • Stock markets
  • Bond markets
  • Derivatives

Key Terms

These six terms will appear throughout every module. Click each to expand its definition.

Capital

Financial resources or assets used to fund operations, make investments, or generate future income.

Risk

The uncertainty associated with an investment's expected returns, or the chance of losing capital.

Return

The financial gain or loss made on an investment over a specific time period.

Liquidity

How easily and quickly an asset can be converted into cash without affecting its market price.

Valuation

The analytical process of determining the current present value of an asset or company.

Yield

The earnings generated and realized on an investment over a particular period of time, expressed as a percentage.
