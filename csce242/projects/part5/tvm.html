Time Value
of Money

The single most important concept in all of finance: a dollar received today is worth more than a dollar received in the future.

1

Why Does Time Affect Value?

Money has time value for three reasons:

Opportunity Cost

Money available today can be invested to earn a return, yielding sacrifices that return.

Inflation

Prices generally rise over time, eroding the purchasing power of future money.

Uncertainty

A payment promised in the future carries risk—it might not materialize. Certain cash now is worth more than uncertain cash later.

The Core Formulas

Future Value
FV = PV × (1 + r)n

Given a present sum PV, grown at r rate for n periods, how much will it be worth in the future?

  • PV = Present Value (starting amount)
  • r = Interest rate per period
  • n = Number of periods
Present Value
PV = FV / (1 + r)n

Given a future sum FV, what is it worth today at rate r? This is called discounting.

  • FV = Future Value (future amount)
  • r = Discount rate
  • n = Number of periods

Net Present Value (NPV)

Most investments produce a series of cash flows over time. NPV sums the present value of all future cash flows and subtracts the initial investment.

NPV = Σ [ CFt / (1 + r)t ] − C0

Where CFt = Cash flow at time t, r = Discount rate, C0 = Initial investment
