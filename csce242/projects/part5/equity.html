What is Equity?

A share of equity (or stock) is a fractional ownership claim on a company's assets and future earnings. Shareholders benefit when the company grows, but they are last in line to be paid in a liquidation — after debt holders.

Unlike debt, equity has no fixed repayment schedule. The return is entirely driven by the company's performance.

Characteristic Debt Equity
Claim Fixed (Interest + Principal) Residual (Profits / Assets)
Priority Senior — paid first Junior — paid last
Maturity Fixed term (or indefinite for perpetual) Perpetual
Governance Limited (Covenants) Voting rights (Board, etc.)
Primary Risk Default Loss of principal
Return Driver Interest rate Business growth + Dividends
CAPITAL STRUCTURE STACK
Senior Secured Debt Lowest Risk / First Paid
Senior Unsecured Debt Moderate Risk
Subordinated Debt Higher Risk
Preferred Stock Hybrid Debt & Equity
Common Equity Highest Risk / Highest Return Potential

Valuation Models

The fundamental principle of equity valuation is the same as bond pricing: the value of a stock equals the present value of all future cash flows it will generate for its owners.

DDM

Dividend Discount Model

Values a stock as the present value of all future dividends, assuming a constant growth rate g.

P = D1 / (r − g)
D1

Next year's dividend

r

Required rate of return

g

Constant dividend growth rate

Requires required return (r) to be strictly greater than dividend growth rate (g).

DCF

Discounted Cash Flow

Values a company by discounting projected free cash flows plus a terminal value back to the present.

P = Σ [ FCFt / (1 + WACC)t ] + TV
FCFt

Free cash flow in period t

r

Discount rate (WACC)

TV

Terminal value at horizon

Used for non-dividend paying companies or when projecting detailed operational forecasts.
