What is Debt?

When an entity — a person, company, or government — needs money, one option is to borrow it. In exchange for cash today, they promise to repay the principal, typically with interest over time. This contract is called a debt instrument.

Debt is the lender's asset and the borrower's liability. The lender earns a return (interest). The borrower gets capital to deploy. Both parties benefit — if the interest rate is priced correctly for the risk.

Understanding Bonds

A bond is a tradable debt instrument. The issuer borrows from investors by selling bonds; investors receive periodic coupon payments (interest) and the face value (principal) at maturity.

Bond Pricing Formula

A bond's price is simply the present value of all its future cash flows — the coupon payments plus the face value — discounted at the market yield (YTM).

P = Σ [ C / (1 + y)t ] + F / (1 + y)n

Where C = coupon payment, y = yield to maturity (YTM), F = face value, n = years to maturity

The Price-Yield Relationship

When market interest rates rise, existing bond prices fall — and vice versa. This inverse relationship is one of the most important in fixed income.
